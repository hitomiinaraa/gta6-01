# EMBER TIDE — Revision Notes

This build is a coastal open-world crime game set in Cinder Cove, a
town that never quite cools down after sunset. The whole mood hangs on
that golden-hour light: long shadows, warm haze over the water, and
streets that glow just before the lamps take over.

The campaign is structured as eight linked story missions, so each job
hands the player a reason to learn a new corner of the map before the
next one raises the stakes. Nothing here is a one-off errand; the chain
is meant to feel like a single evening spiralling outward.

Driving matters as much as shooting. Eight distinct vehicle types give
the player real choices about speed, handling, and how conspicuous
they want to be, and the right pick changes how a getaway actually
plays out rather than just changing the paint job.

Heat is a living system. The wanted scale runs from zero to five, and
at the top end the response escalates to helicopters overhead and
roadblocks cutting across the streets, so laying low becomes a skill
of its own instead of a timer to wait out.

Between jobs the world keeps talking. Four procedural radio stations
fill the drive with different moods, and the audio is generated rather
than looped, so no two cruises sound quite the same.

Navigation was built to stay out of the player's way. A rotating
minimap keeps the heading honest during chases, while the full map
with GPS gives the bigger picture when planning a route across town.

What to check first: that the mission chain flows without a dead end,
that the wanted escalation feels fair at each level, that every
vehicle type is reachable and fun to drive, and that the radio and the
map stay useful when the action gets fast.

One more thing worth watching: the balance between the quiet
golden-hour cruising and the loudest five-star moments. The game
works best when both extremes feel like the same town, just seen
from different sides of the law.
